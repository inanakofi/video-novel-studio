THE LETTER AT THE BLUE GATE

Chapter One: The Return

Ama had not visited the house for three years. Its cream wall was cracked, but the blue gate still scraped the same stone when she pushed it open. She wore the blue coat her mother had given her. In her bag lay the papers that would let her sell the house and leave the street behind.

An envelope was tucked between the bars. Her name was written in her brother Kojo's careful handwriting. Kojo had disappeared on the evening their father died. The police had found his car, his telephone and his suitcase. They had never found him.

Ama opened the envelope. There was no greeting. The letter said, "Look beneath the courtyard lamp before sunset. Bring what you find inside. Come alone."

Chapter Two: The Stone

The lamp stood to the left of the front doorway. Its iron base rested on a square stone. As children, Ama and Kojo had hidden marbles beneath that stone. She remembered how to lift it: pull the chipped corner first, then slide it toward the wall.

Inside the shallow hollow lay a small brass key wrapped in blue cloth. Ama recognized the fabric immediately. Kojo had worn that coat on the night he disappeared. Their mother had repaired one cuff with silver thread, and the same thread crossed the scrap in Ama's hand.

She looked toward the gate. It was still open. Nobody stood in the street. The sun had reached the top of the cream wall, and the courtyard was beginning to fill with shadow.

Chapter Three: The Voice

Ama closed her fist around the key. She had come to sell an empty house. Now someone wanted her to enter it before dark.

"Ama," a voice called from the front room.

It sounded like Kojo. But her brother had never called her Ama when they were alone. He had always used the childhood name their mother had invented.

She stopped at the doorway. "What did you call me when we were little?"

The room fell silent.
